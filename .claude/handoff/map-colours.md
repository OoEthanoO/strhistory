## How colours work now (`src/features/globe/map-colors.ts`)

1. Colour key = `power || pid` (colonies share their empire's `power`).
2. `IDENTITY`: explicit classic colours for ~22 major powers by key (Britain pink, France blue,
   Russia green, …). The user likes these; keep them.
3. else `COUNTRY_COLORS` (`country-colors.ts`, **generated** by `node scripts/data/country-colors.mjs`):
   flag colours of modern countries (Natural Earth `ne:xxx` keys) plus a hand list `HISTORY` of
   predecessor keys. A solver picks pastel shades (OKLCH `SHADES` per colour family) so that
   neighbours stay distinct (OKLab ΔE). It reads IDENTITY and SLOT_PALETTE from map-colors.ts.
4. else one of 12 `SLOT_PALETTE` colours by the record's slot `c`. **This is why the Roman Empire
   is light blue (slot 9, #accffe).**

Charcoal sea `#353535` and stone unclaimed land `#e3e0d9`: avoid near-black and near-stone colours.
AGENTS.md §6 notes that the committed `country-colors.ts` is the Alex's Atlas site's table, and
that a re-run of the generator changes ~120 countries. **The user likes the current colours, so keep
churn of existing assignments minimal** (e.g. make the solver prefer each key's current colour).

## Research results (from the local session's agents)

### Flag-system audit

Verdict: the flag system is wired correctly, but the shipped table was tuned for a different map, so its output is wrong here. Keys match, the regexes parse all 118 IDENTITY keys and all 12 slots, each colour key carries one slot `c`, and the generator uses the same key (power||pid) as mapColor at runtime. Only three keys are dead: the pseudo-codes ne:yug, ne:uar and ne:mfd. Every one of the 185 tier-0 ne: units that isn't an identity colour has a FLAGS entry, and the other 48 ne: keys are dependencies that take their power's colour.

The main problem is that the committed country-colors.ts was solved against the Alex's Atlas reference site's mid-blue sea (#3d6a85) and darker grey land (#a29d92). Running the same script with those two colours reproduces the committed table exactly, with 0 of 277 keys different. With this repo's charcoal sea and stone land (#353535 / #e3e0d9), a re-run changes 118 keys (79 countries). That is the "~120" figure in AGENTS.md, and the theme change, not dataset drift, explains all of it. As a result, 21 keys (Greece 1822–2026, Finland, Indonesia, South Korea, Yemen, Uruguay, Bahrain, Tajikistan and others) are drawn in off-white #ebe8dd. That is ΔE 2.4 from unclaimed land, so they look like no one's land. Another 12 (Ethiopia, Romania, Kazakhstan, DR Congo, Mali, Vietnam and others) sit at ΔE 7.1.

Regenerating alone won't fix this. The candidate shades still include white and very pale yellow and light blue, and the land penalty is too weak. A fresh run still leaves 17 keys within ΔE 8 of the land, including Indonesia, Uruguay and Croatia. The cost weights also give up the flag's first colour too easily: the solver aims for ΔE 14 although the site's own standard is 8, and a family step costs only 6. Coverage is the other big gap. About 40 clear predecessors are missing from HISTORY, for example the Commonwealth of Australia 1905–45 (7.8M km²), Río de la Plata, the Granadine Confederation, the Emirate of Diriyah, the Durrani Empire, Konbaung, the United Principalities (Romania), Ayutthaya, the interwar Baltic states, Ireland and Hungary, and the Papal States. Some identity colours are attached to the wrong keys: Estado Novo Portugal and the 1932–45 Communist Party of China get slot colours, as do Saint-Domingue (through a two-level power chain) and the Dutch possessions in Africa (Cape, Gold Coast) 1803–72.

FLAGS mostly matches the real flags. The exceptions: Egypt is listed yellow-first (its flag is red-white-black; gold is only the eagle), and Finland and Israel are listed light blue (both flags are dark blue). The Roman Empire case the user raised falls outside the flag system, which only reaches states linked to a modern country. Pre-modern powers need IDENTITY entries.
