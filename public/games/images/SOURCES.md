# Historical game image sources

Verified and downloaded on 12 October 2026. This inventory accompanies the 16 rounds in src/features/games/events.ts: four rounds for each of the four selected 2028 studies. This is a source-practice collection, not a statement of complete prescribed syllabus coverage.

The files are authentic archival photographs, prints and paintings. No image is generated. Wikimedia Commons supplies the downloaded JPEGs and documents their reuse status; the linked museums, archives and public institutions support the event facts. The small JPEGs use Commons thumbnails (normally 1280 pixels wide), or the original when smaller. STR History has not recoloured, retouched or generated any image content.

The event date and the artwork's creation date are different fields. All game dates use the Gregorian calendar. Coordinates identify the named building, former building site or grounds; they are reference points for the event location, not surveyed camera positions.

## Reuse and changes

The Enabling Act and book-burning photographs retain the [Creative Commons Attribution–ShareAlike 3.0 Germany licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/de/deed.en). Credit the Bundesarchiv identifier and photographer as listed below, link to the licence, and preserve that licence for adaptations. The book-burning file is an existing Commons crop; its description page links to the original. A source thumbnail may have been resized by Commons. The other works are described as public domain on their linked Commons records; the US Department of Defense photograph is a US government work.

Before a guessing round is submitted, the interface masks the bottom 4% of the Bastille and Italian-parliament images to hide written answer captions. The files themselves remain unchanged, and the complete source appears after the answer is revealed. This inventory and every original source remain available for attribution and inspection.

## Source distinctions and supporting records

- **Charter Oath:** 6 April 1868 corresponds to the fourteenth day of the third month in the contemporary Japanese calendar. [JACAR's chronology](https://www.jacar.go.jp/learning/term.html?uid=Y20C100014) supplies the conversion; the [National Archives of Japan](https://www.archives.go.jp/ayumi/kobetsu/m01_1868_02.html) locates the ceremony at Kyoto Imperial Palace. The painting dates to 1928, rather than 1868.
- **First railway:** the date marks the formal ceremony attended by the emperor at Shimbashi and Yokohama. Limited train services on part of the route had begun earlier. The pictured original Shimbashi site is in Shiodome, not at today's Shimbashi station. [Minato City's anniversary history](https://www.city.minato.tokyo.jp/documents/130746/tetsudo150thbooklet.pdf) also documents the itinerary and site.
- **Meiji Constitution:** the [National Archives of Japan](https://www.archives.go.jp/hub/meiji/meiji02.html) distinguishes promulgation on 11 February 1889 from entry into force on 29 November 1890. [Contemporary diplomatic records in the US Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1889/d223) reproduce the constitution and palace-related accompanying documents.
- **Brandenburg Gate:** this photograph records the gate crossing's opening on 22 December 1989, not the first opening of Berlin Wall checkpoints on 9 November. The gate columns are visible in the selected US Department of Defense photograph.
- **Enabling Act:** the answer is the vote on 23 March 1933, not the law's publication on 24 March. The Kroll Opera House served as the parliament's temporary chamber after the Reichstag fire. Nazi symbols and book-burning imagery are presented as historical evidence, with factual context about coercion and repression.
- **Estates-General:** the engraving was published in 1790 and depicts the opening on 5 May 1789. [Paris Musées' catalogue](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/ouverture-des-etats-generaux-a-versailles-le-5-mai-1789-1) and the [French Ministry of Culture catalogue](https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/joconde/04000013529) support the print's publication date. [Versailles Tourism](https://www.versailles-tourisme.com/l-hotel-des-menus-plaisirs.html) gives coordinates for the site. The hall was a temporary structure behind the Menus-Plaisirs building; it was not the Royal Tennis Court.
- **Festival of the Federation:** the event was 14 July 1790. Commons and a Paris Musées interpretive page date the Thévenin painting to 1796, while the museum's collection listings give 1792. The game therefore credits it conservatively as a painting from the 1790s, made after the event; this artwork-date discrepancy does not affect the event answer.
- **Italian parliament:** the newspaper print illustrates the opening on 18 February 1861 and was published on 16 March. The proclamation of the kingdom followed on 17 March; the [Italian Senate's historical chronology](https://www.senato.it/3380?active_slide_52016=1&testo_generico=640) explicitly separates these dates. The linked study note provides the later proclamation's context.
- **German proclamation:** the ceremony occurred at Versailles, France, on 18 January 1871. The selected 1885 painting was a later commission for Bismarck and is a commemorative construction. Its depiction should not be treated as a photograph made in 1871.
