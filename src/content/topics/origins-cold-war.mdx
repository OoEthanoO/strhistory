## Overview

In 1945 the "Big Three" had defeated Nazi Germany together. Within four years Europe was divided by an <Term id="iron-curtain">iron curtain</Term>, Germany was split into two states, and the United States had signed its first peacetime alliance, <Term id="nato">NATO</Term>. This topic explains how mistrust, ideology and competing security needs turned allies into rivals.

## The breakdown of the alliance

**Ideology.** Capitalist democracy and Soviet communism each saw the other as a long-term threat.

**Security.** After two invasions from the West, Stalin wanted friendly governments in Eastern Europe and a weak Germany. To the West, Soviet control of Poland and the rest of Eastern Europe broke the promise of free elections made at Yalta.

**Germany.** The allies disagreed over reparations and Germany's future. The Western zones were merged and revived economically; the Soviets stripped their zone of industry.

**The atomic bomb.** The US monopoly until 1949 deepened Soviet suspicion.

Stalin compared the speech to Hitler's racial theories. It publicly named the division of Europe and called for Anglo-American partnership against Soviet expansion.">From Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic, an iron curtain has descended across the Continent.</KeyQuote>

## Containment

George Kennan, a US diplomat in Moscow, argued that Soviet behaviour was driven by insecurity and ideology and could be resisted patiently without war. His ideas became the basis of <Term id="containment">containment</Term>.

<KeyQuote by="Harry S. Truman" role="President of the United States" date="12 March 1947" source="Address to Congress (the Truman Doctrine)" analysis="Truman asked Congress for aid to Greece and Turkey, but framed it as a universal commitment. The speech turned containment into public policy and presented the world as divided between free and totalitarian ways of life.">I believe that it must be the policy of the United States to support free peoples who are resisting attempted subjugation by armed minorities or by outside pressures.</KeyQuote>

The Marshall Plan followed: billions of dollars of economic aid to rebuild Western Europe and make communism less attractive. Stalin saw it as dollar imperialism, founded the Cominform, and tightened his grip on Eastern Europe.

## Berlin, 1948–1949

When the Western powers introduced a new currency, the Deutsche Mark, in their zones, Stalin cut all land routes to West Berlin. The United States and Britain supplied the city by air for eleven months. Stalin lifted the blockade in May 1949 — a propaganda defeat that accelerated the creation of NATO and of two German states.

## Historians' debate

<Perspective historian="Herbert Feis" work="From Trust to Terror" year="1970" school="Orthodox">
The orthodox view, dominant in the West in the 1940s and 1950s, blamed Soviet expansionism and saw US policy as a necessary response.
</Perspective>

<Perspective historian="William Appleman Williams" work="The Tragedy of American Diplomacy" year="1959" school="Revisionist">
Williams argued that the US pursuit of an "Open Door" for American trade and investment drove its policy and provoked Soviet fears.
</Perspective>

<Perspective historian="John Lewis Gaddis" work="The United States and the Origins of the Cold War; We Now Know" year="1972; 1997" school="Post-revisionist and after">
Gaddis first stressed mutual misperception and shared responsibility; after Soviet archives opened, he placed renewed emphasis on Stalin's personality and ideology.
</Perspective>

## Test yourself

<Recall q="Why did Poland become a source of conflict between the allies?">
Britain had gone to war over Poland and the West wanted free elections there, as promised at Yalta. Stalin saw a friendly Poland as essential to Soviet security and imposed a communist-dominated government.
</Recall>

<Recall q="How were the Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan linked?">
Both put containment into practice: the Truman Doctrine promised political and military support to countries resisting communism, and the Marshall Plan provided the economic aid to rebuild Europe so that communism would be less attractive.
</Recall>

<Recall q="What were the consequences of the Berlin Blockade?">
It was a Soviet propaganda defeat; it hastened the creation of NATO (April 1949) and the division of Germany into the Federal Republic and the German Democratic Republic; and it confirmed Berlin as a Cold War flashpoint.
</Recall>
