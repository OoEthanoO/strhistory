## Overview

In 1917 Russia went through two revolutions. In February, strikes and mutinies in Petrograd brought down Tsar Nicholas II. The liberal <Term id="provisional-government">Provisional Government</Term> that replaced him shared power with the <Term id="petrograd-soviet">Petrograd Soviet</Term> and kept Russia in the war. In October the <Term id="bolsheviks">Bolsheviks</Term>, led by Lenin and Trotsky, seized power — and then fought a brutal civil war to keep it.

## Why did tsarism collapse?

- **The war.** Defeats and huge casualties, with the tsar personally in command from 1915, tied the monarchy to failure.
- **Economic crisis.** Inflation and food and fuel shortages hit the cities hardest; bread queues in Petrograd turned into strikes.
- **Loss of the elites.** The Rasputin scandal and incompetent ministers alienated the nobility, the Duma and the army's commanders, who advised Nicholas to abdicate.
- **The garrison mutinied.** When soldiers in Petrograd refused to fire on crowds and joined them, the regime had no force left.

## Why did the Provisional Government fail?

The Provisional Government was never elected and shared authority with the Soviet in a system of <Term id="dual-power">dual power</Term>; the Soviet's Order No. 1 gave it effective control over the army. The government kept fighting the war, launched a failed offensive in June, and postponed land reform and elections to a Constituent Assembly. Each delay made the Bolsheviks' simple slogans more attractive.

<KeyQuote by="Vladimir Lenin" role="Bolshevik leader" date="April 1917" source="Bolshevik slogans after the April Theses" analysis="The slogans promised exactly what the Provisional Government was failing to deliver — an end to the war, land for the peasants and food for the cities — and demanded that the soviets, not the government, rule.">Peace, land and bread! All power to the soviets!</KeyQuote>

The Kornilov affair was the turning point: Kerensky armed the Petrograd workers — including Bolshevik Red Guards — to stop an apparent military coup, and the Bolsheviks won majorities in the Petrograd and Moscow soviets. Trotsky's Military Revolutionary Committee organised the seizure of the capital on 25 October. When moderate socialists walked out of the Congress of Soviets in protest, Trotsky told them to go <Q by="Leon Trotsky" date="25 October 1917" source="Second Congress of Soviets" note="A famous dismissal of the Mensheviks and Socialist Revolutionaries — and a sign that the Bolsheviks intended to rule alone rather than share power with other socialists.">into the dustbin of history</Q>.

## Holding on to power, 1917–1924

- **Decrees** on peace and land won support; the <Term id="constituent-assembly">Constituent Assembly</Term>, where the Socialist Revolutionaries had won most seats, was dissolved when it would not accept Bolshevik rule.
- **Brest-Litovsk** ended the war at the cost of huge territory, but gave the regime breathing space.
- **The Civil War** was won through the Red Army's discipline under Trotsky, control of the central railway network and industrial heartland, and the divisions and foreign links of the Whites.
- **Terror and War Communism** — grain requisitioning, nationalisation, and the Cheka — kept the regime in power at enormous human cost.
- **Retreat to the NEP** after the Kronstadt rising showed Lenin's pragmatism: private trade returned while the party banned factions within itself.

## Historians' debate

<Perspective historian="Richard Pipes" work="The Russian Revolution" year="1990" school="Liberal / 'totalitarian'">
Pipes saw October as a coup by a small, conspiratorial party, not a popular revolution, and traced Stalinist terror back to Lenin.
</Perspective>

<Perspective historian="Sheila Fitzpatrick" work="The Russian Revolution" year="1982" school="Revisionist / social history">
Fitzpatrick emphasised genuine popular radicalism among workers, soldiers and peasants in 1917, and treated the revolution as a longer process lasting into the 1930s.
</Perspective>

<Perspective historian="Orlando Figes" work="A People's Tragedy" year="1996" school="Synthesis">
Figes combined high politics with ordinary people's experience, arguing that the revolution grew from popular grievances but was captured by a party whose methods turned it into a tragedy.
</Perspective>

<Recall q="What was 'dual power', and why did it weaken the Provisional Government?">
The division of authority between the Provisional Government and the Petrograd Soviet in 1917. The Soviet controlled key resources — through Order No. 1, the army's obedience — so the government could not act without its agreement.
</Recall>

<Recall q="Why was the Kornilov affair a turning point?">
Kerensky relied on the soviets and armed Red Guards to stop Kornilov. The Bolsheviks appeared as defenders of the revolution, their support surged, and they won majorities in the Petrograd and Moscow soviets.
</Recall>

<Recall q="Give three reasons the Reds won the Civil War.">
Central position and control of railways and industry; a disciplined, centrally commanded Red Army; the Whites' divisions, reactionary image and dependence on foreign support. Terror and grain requisitioning also kept the Reds supplied and suppressed opposition.
</Recall>
