## What happened?

- On April 22nd 1915, Allied troops were defending the town of Ypres in Belgium, a key city for transporting supplies and troops from the UK
- Holding this <Term id="line">line</Term> lead to a <Term id="salient">salient</Term> being formed around the city of Ypres, a challenging military situation for the Allies
- During the battle, the Germans unleashed 5730 canisters containing 160 tonnes of chlorine gas against Allied positions, which was a <Term id="war-crime">war crime</Term>

<figure>
  <img src="/g10-curriculum/Ypres1.png" alt="German troops releasing chlorine gas cylinders" />
  <figcaption>German troops releasing chlorine gas canisters across Allied positions.</figcaption>
</figure>

- The chlorine gas formed HCl acid in the Allied soldiers’ lungs, drowning their lungs in acid, forcing French and Algerian forces to retreat 6km
- Faced with the collapse of the <Term id="line">line</Term>, Canadian troops filled in the gap and heroically fended off the Germans for 3 days, even counterattacking the Germans

<figure>
  <img src="/g10-curriculum/Ypres3.png" alt="Canadian troops hold the line at Ypres" />
  <figcaption>Canadian troops hold the line at Ypres</figcaption>
</figure>

- Faced with further gas attacks, Canadian army medics recommended urine soaked cloths as temporary gas masks, but many Canadian soldiers would face lifelong respiratory troubles from the chlorine gas

<figure>
  <img src="/g10-curriculum/Ypres2.png" alt="Soldiers moving through gas with early respirators" />
  <figcaption>Soldiers moving through trenches enveloped in poison gas with early respiratory protection.</figcaption>
</figure>

- 6,036 Canadian <Term id="casualty">casualties</Term> amongst 60,000 British <Term id="casualty">casualties</Term> against 34,873 German <Term id="casualty">casualties</Term>

- After the battle, the famous poem <Term id="flanders-fields">Flanders Fields</Term> was composed by John McCrae

## Why is this important?

- The victory of Canadian arms, previously considered untested and secondary in quality to British troops, was a resounding testament to the gallantry of Canadian troops
- It was the beginning of a series of engagements in which the equal or superior performance of Canadian troops would warrant them greater autonomy in the British army
- It proclaimed to the world for the first time the strength of Canada, going from amateurs considered “unsuitable for battle” to defeating a world-class European army in less than twelve months
- The poem <Term id="flanders-fields">Flanders Fields</Term> would become an icon of Remembrance Day, commemorating initially the heroic sacrifice of Canadian soldiers at Ypres and later, in battle across the world
