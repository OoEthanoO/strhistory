## Overview

The Bastille fell on 14 July 1789 amid fears of royal repression and food shortages. A crowd seeking gunpowder confronted the fortress's defenders. After fighting and negotiations, the garrison surrendered. Only seven prisoners were inside; the event's later significance cannot be explained by the number released.

<Chronology>
  <Event date="14 July, morning">Crowds obtained weapons at the Invalides.</Event>
  <Event date="14 July, afternoon">The Bastille's garrison surrendered.</Event>
</Chronology>

These details are drawn from the [French presidency's historical account](https://www.elysee.fr/en/french-presidency/bastille-day-14-july).

## Why did it matter?

The fortress was associated with royal power under the <Term id="ancien-regime">ancien régime</Term>. Its capture joined a practical demand for ammunition to a visible challenge to authority. The event also showed that political change involved people outside the deputies' meeting rooms.

Separating motive from consequence prevents a common error: an event can gain symbolic importance beyond the immediate purpose of its participants.

## Interpreting the evidence

The [Palace of Versailles preserves an eighteenth-century painting](https://en.chateauversailles.fr/node/26234) by an anonymous artist depicting the surrender. An image offers evidence of how the event was represented; its dramatic composition should not automatically be treated as a neutral record of everything that happened.

Compare the image's treatment of crowds, defenders and buildings. Then ask what written testimony or material evidence would be needed to check its details. This is a source exercise, not a claim that the artist's intentions can be known without further evidence.
