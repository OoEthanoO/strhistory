## Overview

The <Term id="charter-oath">Charter Oath</Term> was issued early in the Meiji Restoration. It presented aims for government after the fall of the Tokugawa shogunate, using the emperor's authority to announce a programme of change.

<Chronology>
  <Event date="1868">The new government displaced the Tokugawa shogunate.</Event>
  <Event date="April 1868">The Charter Oath announced the new government's aims.</Event>
</Chronology>

The [National Archives of Japan](https://www.archives.go.jp/ayumi/kobetsu/m01_1868_02.html) records the ceremony at Kyoto's imperial palace. Its date uses the contemporary Japanese calendar.

## Promises of change

<KeyQuote by="Emperor Meiji's government" date="April 1868" source="Charter Oath, article 5; Sources of Japanese Tradition, edited by de Bary, Tsunoda and Keene (1964), vol. 2, p. 137; reproduced by Columbia University" analysis="The statement connected learning from other countries to strengthening imperial authority. Reform and continuity could serve the same political purpose.">Knowledge shall be sought throughout the world so as to strengthen the foundations of imperial rule.</KeyQuote>

The [translation and contextual introduction at Columbia University](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps/ps_japan-charter-oath-1868.htm) identify the reforming samurai behind the text. An imperial voice should therefore not be confused with personal authorship of every phrase.

## Interpreting the evidence

The oath's reforming language reveals intentions and a public attempt to define the new regime. It does not alone establish who gained practical influence or whether promised consultation occurred.

A useful inquiry would compare the announcement with later institutions and experiences. The [National Archives account of the deliberative assembly](https://www.archives.go.jp/exhibition/digital/henbou/contents/06.html) provides one route for following a stated aim into an institutional experiment.
