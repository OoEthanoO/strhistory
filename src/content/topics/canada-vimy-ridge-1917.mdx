## What happened?

- After months of reorganization, the four divisions of the Canadian Corps were allowed to fight together instead of as detachment units of British troops
- The goal for the Canadian Corps was to seize Vimy Ridge, a highly fortified position as part of a larger Allied offensive against Arras
- Vimy Ridge was one of the most formidable defensive positions on the Western Front, with complex tunnels, trenches defended by machine guns and artillery
- Allied assaults on the ridge in 1914 and 1915 had been repulsed with heavy <Term id="casualty">casualties</Term> and no success
- In order to succeed this time, the Canadian army made extensive preparations for the upcoming offensive, doing the following
  1. Digging tunnels for secure command posts, medical stations and communications
  2. Scale models of the terrain to instruct officers as to where to lead their troops, up to a full scale replica
  3. Use of aerial reconnaissance to create 40,000 maps
- The Canadians were helped by recent technological development, which included
  - Aerial dogfighting to win air superiority
  - <Term id="tank">Tanks</Term> deployed in larger scale
  - Portable Lewis Gun to transform the machine gun from a stationary defensive to a mobile offensive weapon by protecting troops on the attack
- Before the battle, the German Army was subjected to 13 days of ‘trommel feuer’ or drumfire, artillery fire so dense and loud it resembled a drumbeat
- During the battle, Canadian artillerymen perfected the creeping barrage, in which artillery would constantly fire just ahead of the advancing infantry to provide continuous artillery cover
- Amongst the officers included Arthur Currie commanding the 1st Division, soon to be promoted to full commander of the Canadian Corps
- When the actual battle arrived, the extensive preparations lead to a decisive Canadian victory with 3 of 4 objectives achieved in 2 hours, which previously 2 years of offensives could not achieve
- The Nova Scotia Highlanders in a feat of extraordinary daring, seized Hill 145 without any artillery support
- Despite the victory, it was the bloodiest day in Canadian military history was 7,000 wounded and 3,598 killed

## Why is it important?

- The victory at Vimy Ridge became and continues to be a defining moment of national pride for Canadian identity
- Fighting alone with their fellow countrymen for the first time, the four Canadian divisions had shown the world what Canadian unity, ingenuity and bravery could achieve
- After months of slaughter on the Somme, the Canadians had revolutionized their tactics and planning to fit a fully modern war, showing the extraordinary adaptability of Canadian military minds
- The victories achieved at Vimy solidified the argument that Canadian soldiers and the Canadian nation was not inferior to their British overlords
- The success of the Canadian Corps in this battle cemented their reputation as the elite, dependable shock troops who could be counted on to overwhelm even the most resolute of defenses
- Vimy was truly a coming to age moment of Canada, where the glory of this victory became inseparable from the emerging Canadian consciousness
- That Canadians had stood, fought and prevailed against the full might of Germany showed that Canada deserved a place in the world reflective of its increasing military prowess
