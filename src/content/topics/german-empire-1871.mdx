## Overview

On 18 January 1871, the Prussian king Wilhelm I was proclaimed German emperor at Versailles. The location placed a statement of national unity inside the defeated opponent's royal palace while the Franco-Prussian War continued. The pin belongs in France because it marks where this particular event occurred.

The [German Historical Museum's constitutional history](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/kaiserreich/reich/verfassung) distinguishes these stages.

## What kind of unity?

The empire was a <Term id="federal-state">federal state</Term>: its constituent states survived within a common political structure. Prussian leadership did not erase every regional institution. The settlement incorporated particular rights negotiated by southern states.

The [museum's account of the Versailles proclamation](https://www.dhm.de/archiv/ausstellungen/bismarck/doku/html/raum8c.html) places it within the union of the North German Confederation and the southern states under Prussian predominance.

## Interpreting the evidence

A ceremony makes authority visible but cannot explain the entire process that produced it. Assessing unification requires evidence about military outcomes, diplomatic agreements and constitutional powers as well as national symbolism.

Compare this event with [Italy's proclamation in 1861](/topics/kingdom-italy-1861). Both new states retained monarchies. The relevant comparison is how each monarchy related to existing institutions and political participation, not simply whether a king appeared at a ceremony.
