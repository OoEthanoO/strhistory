## Extra: Canadian identity before the war

- Canadians in 1914 saw Canada as
  - Ont/Maritimes = proud to be part of the British Empire
    - Canadian Imperialists
    - Identity came from the Brits
    - No Canadian spirit yet
  - Prairie states = want to be independent from the Brits
    - Want to be more connected to the United States
    - Continentalism
      - Want to embrace the econ. connection with the United States
      - Political unification will follow economic unification
  - Quebec = want to be apart from the rest of canada
    - Isolationism
      - They are a minority so they want to protect what they have through staying with their people
      - Have to keep having babies to survive in the west
      - Want nothing to do with the British or the French
      - Don’t want to engage in ww1
      - Made laws with the language that forced the wealthy english speakers to leave Quebec

## What happened?

- Following Britain’s declaration on August 4th, Canada, as a <Term id="dominion">DOMINION</Term> of the British Empire, was automatically brought into the First World War

<figure>
  <img src="/g10-curriculum/CanadaWarDeclaration3.png" alt="The Quebec Chronicle headline: England Now At War" />
  <figcaption><em>The Quebec Chronicle</em> headline on 5 August 1914 announcing Britain’s entry into the war.</figcaption>
</figure>

## Why is it important?

- This event shows that in 1914, Canadian <Term id="sovereignty">sovereignty</Term> was still largely limited as Canada could not control its interactions with other nations
- It was represented solely by Britain on the international stage despite having control over it’s internal affairs
- The lack of <Term id="foreign-affairs">foreign affairs</Term> shows that Canada was still not fully equal on a country-country basis with European nations despite fighting alongside them
