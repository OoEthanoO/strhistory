## Extra: Canadian identity before the war

- Canadians in 1914 disagreed about what Canada was and where its loyalties lay
  - Ontario and the Maritimes = many were proud to be part of the British Empire
    - Canadian imperialists
    - Their identity came largely from Britain
    - They saw Canada’s future as a strong partner within the Empire
  - The Prairies = many wanted closer ties with the United States
    - Continentalism
      - Wanted to embrace economic ties with the United States; Wilfrid Laurier’s Liberals lost the 1911 election after promising free trade (reciprocity)
      - Opponents feared that political union would follow economic union
  - Quebec = many French Canadians wanted Canada to stay out of Britain’s wars
    - Isolationism
      - As a minority, they wanted to protect their language, Catholic faith and culture
      - Large families had helped French Canada survive as a minority
      - Felt little loyalty to Britain or to France
      - Many saw no reason to fight in a British war overseas
      - Ontario’s Regulation 17 (1912), which restricted French-language schooling in Ontario, angered many French Canadians

## What happened?

- Following Britain’s declaration on August 4th, Canada, as a <Term id="dominion">Dominion</Term> of the British Empire, was automatically brought into the First World War
- Canada could not stay neutral, but its own government decided how much to contribute: the cabinet approved an expeditionary force on August 6th, and a special session of Parliament passed the War Measures Act on August 22nd

## Why is it important?

- This event shows that in 1914, Canadian <Term id="sovereignty">sovereignty</Term> was still largely limited as Canada could not control its interactions with other nations
- It was represented solely by Britain on the international stage despite having control over its internal affairs
- Its lack of control over <Term id="foreign-affairs">foreign affairs</Term> shows that Canada was still not fully equal, country to country, with European nations despite fighting alongside them
