## Overview

The historical investigation is a research project of **up to 2,200 words** on a historical question you choose. It is marked by your teacher and moderated by the IB, and is worth **25% at Standard Level and 20% at Higher Level**. The bibliography is not included in the word count.

| Section | Marks | Suggested words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Identification and evaluation of sources | 6 | about 500 |
| 2. Investigation | 15 | about 1,300 |
| 3. Reflection | 4 | about 400 |

## Choosing a question

A good question is **narrow, arguable and answerable** with sources you can actually get. "Why did the Weimar Republic fail?" is too broad; "To what extent did the 1923 hyperinflation weaken support for the Weimar Republic among the middle classes?" gives you a focus.

## Section 1: identification and evaluation of sources

State your question clearly, then analyse **two sources** that are relevant to it in detail. Explain why each is relevant and evaluate its value and limitations with reference to its **origin, purpose and content** — the same skills as Paper 1.

## Section 2: investigation

This is your argument. It should be well organised, use a range of sources, show critical analysis, and reach a reasoned conclusion that answers the question. Consider different perspectives.

## Section 3: reflection

Reflect on what the investigation taught you about **the methods historians use and the challenges they face** — for example, the problem of selecting evidence, the reliability of memoirs, or how historians' own contexts shape interpretations. Connect your reflection to your own investigation.

<Callout type="tip">
Keep a research log from day one: where each source came from, what it says, and what you thought about it. It makes section 1 and the reflection far easier to write.
</Callout>
