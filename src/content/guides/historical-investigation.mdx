## The revised investigation

For first assessment 2028, the investigation has three sections: **historical inquiry question**, **sources and perspectives**, and **synthesis and evaluation**. The former reflection section has been removed, and the investigation may use at most seven sources. These changes are confirmed in the [IB's History curriculum update](https://ibo.org/university-admission/latest-curriculum-updates/history-updates/).

The [SL](/courses/ib-history-sl) and [HL](/courses/ib-history-hl) pages give the current assessment weights. Use the teacher's current guide for word limits, detailed criteria and submission requirements; the previous guide's section allocations should not be reused.

## Build a question that can be answered

Choose a bounded event or development. Define time and place, identify a historical problem, and check that accessible sources can support more than a descriptive account. A source search may change the question; record why it changed.

## Select sources for a purpose

Keep a research log with full references, page numbers, relevant evidence and limitations. Select sources because of what they contribute to the inquiry. Several sources repeating the same account do not necessarily provide independent corroboration.

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep quotations traceable">
Record exact wording and translation details when taking notes. Mark paraphrases separately so they cannot become accidental quotations later.
</Callout>

## Turn evidence into an argument

Group evidence around competing explanations. Explain what each source can establish, consider tensions between accounts and reach a judgement proportionate to the evidence. Distinguish an absence of evidence from evidence that something did not happen.
