## The exams

- **Paper 2** (SL and HL): 1 hour 30 minutes, two essays, each on a **different** world history topic. Each essay is marked out of 15.
- **Paper 3** (HL only): 2 hours 30 minutes, three essays on your regional option. Each essay is marked out of 15.

That is roughly **45 minutes per essay** in both papers.

## What examiners reward

The highest markbands describe essays that are **clearly focused** on the question, **well structured**, supported by **accurate and relevant knowledge**, **critically analytical** throughout, and that **evaluate different perspectives**. Narrative — telling the story — is the most common reason strong knowledge earns weak marks.

## Before you write: five minutes of planning

1. **Unpack the command term.** "To what extent" and "evaluate" ask for a judgement; "compare and contrast" needs both similarities and differences; "discuss" asks for a balanced review.
2. **Find the key concept.** Is the question about causes, consequences, change, continuity or significance?
3. **Decide your answer** before you start. Your thesis should answer the question in one or two sentences.
4. **Plan three or four analytical paragraphs**, each making a point that supports or qualifies your thesis.

## Paragraph structure

A reliable pattern is **point, evidence, analysis, link**:

- **Point:** a topic sentence that makes an argument, not a statement of fact.
- **Evidence:** specific, accurate detail — names, dates, figures, events.
- **Analysis:** explain *how* the evidence supports the point. This is where marks are won.
- **Link:** connect back to the question and your overall judgement.

<Callout type="historiography" title="Using historians">
Historians' views strengthen an essay when you use them as evidence in your argument ("Crowley's emphasis on security helps explain…") rather than listing them. Perspectives can also come from contemporaries, nations or social groups — not only named historians.
</Callout>

## The conclusion

Answer the question. Weigh your factors and reach a clear judgement that follows from your paragraphs — no new material.

<Callout type="warning">
Don't write everything you know about a topic. Every sentence should help answer *this* question.
</Callout>
