## The revised paper

The department studies **Political and economic transitions**. Paper 1 centres on an inquiry question and three sources. Its three questions concern relevant content, a source's context and perspectives across the source set. This replaces the earlier four-question structure. See the [SL](/courses/ib-history-sl) and [HL](/courses/ib-history-hl) pages for timings and assessment weights.

These changes are explained in the [IB's History curriculum update](https://ibo.org/university-admission/latest-curriculum-updates/history-updates/).

## Read for the inquiry

Start by identifying the question's time, place and central issue. A relevant detail is evidence that helps answer that question, not simply an interesting phrase. Link each selected detail to the inference it supports.

## Explain context

Ask who produced a source, when, for which audience and under what pressures. Then explain how that context affects its use. A government statement can reveal an intended policy without proving that the policy succeeded.

<Callout type="tip" title="Make the connection explicit">
Use a specific feature of the source to explain what it helps establish and what remains uncertain. Calling a document biased is only the beginning of an argument.
</Callout>

## Bring perspectives together

Compare how sources frame the same issue. Identify agreements, disagreements and differences of emphasis. Explain whether these reflect different experiences, purposes or access to information. Use relevant contextual knowledge to test an interpretation.
