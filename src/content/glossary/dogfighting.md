---
term: Dogfighting
when: 1915–present
definition: Aerial combat between fighter aircraft at close range, with pilots manoeuvring to gain a firing position behind the enemy.
significance: Control of the air above a battlefield became crucial in the First World War. Before Vimy Ridge, Canadian and British aircraft flew missions to deny German observers a view of Canadian preparations, preventing the enemy from seeing the scale of what was coming. Air superiority — winning the dogfights — directly contributed to the surprise and success of the attack.
