---
term: Over the Top
when: 1914–1918
definition: Leaving the safety of the trenches to cross no man’s land and attack the enemy’s trenches. Attacking troops were highly vulnerable, and many were cut down moments after climbing out.
significance: Going over the top exposed soldiers to machine-gun fire, artillery, and barbed wire with almost no cover. On the first day of the Somme alone, 57,470 British soldiers became casualties — many within moments of leaving their trenches. The phrase became a byword for suicidal futility.
